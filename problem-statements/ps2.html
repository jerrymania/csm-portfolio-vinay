Material Procurement, Storage and Quality Control

A traceable procurement-to-storage system that a new store manager can run, starting 15 days before work begins.

Source-basedBased on supplied workshop records

On this pageProblemMeetingsCycleGatesInvoicesStoreQA / QCRejectionOutcomeEvidence
01

The problem

Develop a material procurement, quality-control, storage and accounts system that runs with minimum day-to-day supervision from the project manager. The newly hired store manager has no large-project experience, and work starts in 15 days Source.

  1. Prepare a store layout for consumables, non-consumables, equipment and safety gear.
  2. Decide which of the two gates to use for material ingress and departure, based on store location and site layout.
  3. Record vehicle registration numbers at entry and exit.
  4. The driver brings the invoice; copies go to security, store manager and accounts.
  5. Inspect incoming material with the QA/QC inspector and store manager, using the nearby material-testing lab.
  6. If rejected, remove the material from the premises promptly and keep rejection records.
  7. Move accepted material to its designated storage location and keep records.

05

Invoice and document control

Copy 1Security — retained at the gate
Copy 2Store manager — checked against the PO
Copy 3Accounts — matched before payment
  1. Security checks the delivery document exists and records its reference.
  2. Store manager checks PO, item, unit, quantity and supplier.
  3. QA/QC records the inspection and test disposition.
  4. Store manager raises a goods-received note (GRN) for the accepted quantity.
  5. Accounts matches PO, invoice and GRN.
  6. Any mismatch, damage, shortage or rejection is recorded and escalated.
  7. A traceable physical / digital record is kept, with access control and periodic audit.
An invoice alone does not prove that material was accepted or what quantity was received; only the GRN after inspection does.

FIFO is used for shelf-life-sensitive items, but not blindly where batch-specific engineering or specification requirements dictate otherwise. The written answers also cover accessibility, organisation, handling, inventory, safety, climate, security and future expansion.

08

Rejection and non-conformance

  1. Identify non-conforming material
  2. Stop unloading / segregate safely
  3. Tag and quarantine
  4. Record supplier, batch, qty, defect, photos, evidence
  5. Notify procurement, PM and supplier
  6. Written return / replacement / concession
  7. Controlled removal from site
  8. Update inventory and accounts
  9. Close after authorised resolution

The assignment says rejected material should leave the premises immediately. That stays the requirement, but safe handling, documentation and an authorised return arrangement still apply.

09

Outcome and learning

The proposed system gives traceability from purchase order through delivery, inspection, storage, issue and reconciliation. Clear responsibilities, controlled records and exception reporting reduce the need for constant project-manager intervention. It is a proposal: no implementation or savings are claimed.
