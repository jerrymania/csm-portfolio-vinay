Concrete Quality Assurance and Non-Conformance Management

A traceable plan for checking concrete from supplier approval to cube testing, with a documented path for anything out of specification.

Constructed case studyConstructed academic case study — prepared to complete the portfolio; not represented as an original submitted problem statement.

On this pageScenarioWorkflowChecksCube recordNCRMinutesConclusionEvidence
01

Scenario

Develop a concrete QA/QC plan for a multi-block mall with an estimated demand of about 12,000 m³ (PS1), so that concrete materials and delivered concrete comply with approved mix designs and project specifications.

Verify incoming cement, aggregates, water and admixtures
Check delivery documents and approved mix details
Control fresh concrete at the point of placement
Keep samples and results traceable
Manage non-conforming concrete through engineering review
Use records to prevent recurrence
02

Quality-control workflow

  1. Approve suppliers and mix designs
  2. Verify ticket: grade, batch time, qty, mix ID
  3. Inspect fresh concrete; specified tests
  4. Record location, time, truck / batch, sample ID
  5. Cast, identify, cure, test per standards and quality plan
  6. Compare with acceptance criteria
  7. Record NCRs; refer structural decisions to engineer
  8. Close CAPA with evidence
03

Typical checks

Material / workPossible checkQualification
CementManufacturer certificate, visual condition, specified lab testsTest selection depends on specification and standard
AggregatesGrading, moisture, cleanliness, specified physical propertiesSampling and limits follow the approved specification
Fresh concreteSlump / workability, temperature where specified, visual uniformitySlump is not a direct measure of compressive strength
Hardened concreteCompressive-strength specimens at specified agesNo results or acceptance conclusions are invented
ReinforcementGrade / diameter certificates, dimensions, specified testsTraceable to batch and approved drawings

The PS2 source lists cement consistency and setting time, slump, fineness modulus, compaction, flakiness / elongation and compression tests. They are tests named in that source, not tests carried out on this project.

05

Non-conformance and corrective action

  1. Quarantine records; identify pour / batch
  2. Notify QA/QC lead and engineer
  3. Review sampling, curing, calibration, traceability
  4. Assess structural significance (approved procedure)
  5. Further testing only by authorised direction
  6. Document disposition, CA and PA
  7. Close after review and approval
Principle
A low cube result doesn't automatically mean demolition, and a passing individual cube doesn't prove the whole structure complies.

07

Conclusion

The case sets out a traceable QA/QC framework for concrete production, delivery, placement and testing. No test result, cube strength, failure, rejection or structural disposition is claimed.
