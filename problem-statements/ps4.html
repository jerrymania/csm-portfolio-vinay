Construction Labour Planning and Welfare

Forecasting, deploying and looking after the workforce as the mall's work fronts change.

Constructed case studyConstructed academic case study — prepared to complete the portfolio; not represented as an original submitted problem statement.

On this pageScenarioMethodWelfareDashboardMinutesConclusionEvidence
01

Scenario

For the same multi-block mall, develop a workforce planning and welfare system. It should coordinate skilled, semi-skilled and unskilled labour with changing work fronts, while keeping accommodation, access and welfare safe and productivity under control.

This extends PS1's labour-housing requirement as a learning exercise. The source gives no worker headcount Context.

Forecast labour demand by trade and phase
Match deployment to approved work packages
Reduce idle time, overcrowding and congestion
Provide adequate welfare and safe access
Keep attendance, competency and induction records
Monitor productivity without compromising safety or quality
02

Planning method

  1. Break programme into work packages
  2. Identify trades per package
  3. Estimate crews from quantities, approved norms, hours
  4. Weekly labour histogram
  5. Compare planned vs actual attendance
  6. Reallocate only after readiness, supervision, safety checks
  7. Review and update weekly

Work packages: excavation, foundations, basement works, frame, masonry, services and finishes.

MethodCrew-days = Work quantity ÷ Approved productivity norm → Crew size = Crew-days ÷ Available working days
No worker counts or productivity rates are inserted: none were supplied.
03

Welfare and safety

Drinking water and sanitation
Clean, ventilated accommodation where provided
Rest areas and first aid
PPE issue and replacement records
Site induction and task training
Emergency contacts and evacuation
Pedestrian routes separated from heavy vehicles
Housekeeping, waste disposal, pest control
Grievance and incident reporting
Check before use
All legal requirements must be checked against current legislation and the actual project conditions. No compliance is claimed.

06

Conclusion

A labour plan should be dynamic and tied to the construction programme. This case shows how forecasting, welfare, competency and safety controls support reliable execution. It does not claim a workforce was deployed on the source project.
